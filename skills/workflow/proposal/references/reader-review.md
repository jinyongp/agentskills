# Recipient review

Before delivery, review only the draft and the attachments or sources the recipient
will actually receive. Set aside earlier conversation and author notes for this
pass. Check whether the reader can understand the need, justified expectation,
constraints, evidence limits, requested decision, and next verification step.

## Check context and relevance

- **Select useful context.** Locate names, paths, shorthand, and references such as "the project" or "as
  agreed" whose meaning depends on private context. Explain their role when it
  changes assessment; remove incidental identifiers and work history.
- **Preserve decisive detail.** Keep concrete details that affect validity, reproduction, scope, or preservation.
  Do not generalize away a meaningful distinction or rewrite recorded commands as
  though a simplified example had been executed. Label illustrative examples.
- **Diagnose a passage.** Check whether each concern changes a reader's understanding or next action.
  Report a specific ambiguity and its consequence; stylistic preferences and
  missing optional detail do not justify new requirements or investigations.

An internal job name is usually replaceable by its role. Do not demand the
consumer's full configuration merely because a command names that job. Transfer
the triggering conditions needed for assessment; include exact commands when
their identity matters to evidence or an actual reproduction. If a missing source
or condition blocks a claim, obtain it, qualify that claim, or name its recovery
path. A general statement that the draft was reviewed does not resolve the gap.

For example, a staff nickname for a weekly equipment review adds no useful meaning
unless the recipient knows it; describe the review's purpose and timing instead.
In contrast, a deadline measured in business days must retain that distinction
because replacing it with "days" changes the proposed behavior. These examples
illustrate relevance decisions, not required proposal content.

## Choose the review method

Use a separate reader subagent when cross-team transfer or heavy reliance on prior
context makes a fresh reading useful, and delegation is available and authorized.
Keep the task bounded: give it only the draft, intended audience, requested decision,
and recipient-accessible attachments. Do not give prior conversation, author notes,
expected findings, or permission to implement or submit. Ask for consequential
ambiguities with exact passages and reasons, without a finding quota. Respect the
host's model and delegation rules; no particular model is required.

Otherwise perform the same recipient pass yourself, and do not describe it as
independent validation. A reader's inability to verify a claim is an evidence gap,
not proof that the claim is false.

After either pass, compare proposed edits with the original sources. Repair
supported context gaps without adding assumptions, deleting necessary evidence,
or expanding scope. Re-read affected passages; leave unresolved material gaps
explicit. Return the revised proposal, with review limits only when consequential,
rather than appending an internal review transcript by default.
