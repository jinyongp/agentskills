# Success criteria by surface

Choose priorities from what the visitor should accomplish on the selected surface,
not the product's category. A tool can have a promotional landing page and a task
interface; their priorities differ. Mixed surfaces can need several criteria.

| Visitor goal | Inspect | Design decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decide whether to adopt, buy, or sign up | Can the visitor identify the offer, relevant evidence, material conditions, and next action? | Group supported reasons beside the decision; keep commitment conditions visible and distinguish competing actions |
| Complete a task | Can the user locate the next control, understand current state, and reach completion or applicable recovery? | Favor predictable controls, readable state, and task-relevant density; keep visual expression from obscuring operation |
| Understand or look up information | Do the opening, headings, examples, and navigation expose the answer and its qualifications? | Keep explanation in reading order, link separate depth, and distinguish code examples from supporting mechanics |
| Examine creative work | Can the visitor inspect the work at useful size, find its context, and navigate it with supported inputs? | Let the work carry attention; retain discoverable navigation and accessible alternatives |

Use these questions for the affected journey and reachable states. Preserve requested
identity and existing contracts. A clear route to purchase does not justify fabricated
claims; a beautiful resting view does not prove task completion. Source review,
rendered inspection, and observed user comprehension provide different evidence.
